# Week 5: Evaluating an Agent Environment

*See this week's tutorial, where Sheer built a [simulated environment of a student's laptop](https://github.com/skarny0/learnos).*

In previous weeks, you built an agent loop, expanded it with tools, and designed how it interacts with a human. This week, you will develop an evaluation of your environment from week 4 that tells you whether the agent actually achieves its objective.

## 1. Define meaningful scores

Define **at least two distinct evaluation measures** for your environment from week 4. Your evaluation must include:
- **At least one deterministic/programmatic score** tied to the environment state or trajectory.
- **At least one additional measure** appropriate to your problem.
For each measure, identify:
1. **What is being evaluated?** Final output, action/tool call, complete trajectory, final environment state, or whole episode.
2. **How is it scored?** Deterministic/programmatic scorer, model-based scorer, human evaluation, or another justified procedure.
3. **Does this measure evaulate if the agent achieved its objective well? Why/why not?**

## 2. Create a task suite and instrument it with Langfuse

For your chosen metrics, create **at least 5 task instances for your environment** where an end environment state is the objective. Use **Langfuse** to instrument your evaluation.

## 3. Run experiments

Run your agent across the task suite. If either the agent policy or environment is stochastic, run **multiple trials per task** so that your results are not based on a single lucky or unlucky trajectory (pass@k, pass^k).
Report aggregate performance, but also inspect some individual traces. Select one interesting success or failure trace and explain what happened. Where did it succeed? Where did it fail?
Add **at least one plot** or **table** summarizing important results from your evaluation.

## 4. Document your progress

Overall, in your Week 5 documentation, include:
- **Evaluation:** Your metrics, units of evaluation, scorer types, and why they capture the intended objective, according to criteria in (1).
- **Task suite and Langfuse:** Your task instances, experiment setup, traces, and scores.
- **Results:** Show aggregate results.
- **Failure analysis:** What went wrong and where in the agent-environment loop the failures occurred.
- A link to your code and an example run
- Images or video documentation.
- A short reflection on what you learned and what you would improve.
- A disclosure of how and why you used AI.
As in previous weeks, your documentation and reflection should be written by you rather than generated by AI. AI coding assistance is allowed as long as you understand and can explain the resulting system.

## 5. Submission and in-class sharing

Publish your Week 5 documentation and code to the private GitHub repository associated with the GitHub ID you provided to the course staff. Test the links and demo media from a signed-out or fresh browser session where possible.
You may be randomly selected to present your week 4 and 5 work in class. Be ready to explain:
1. **The problem:** What objectives is the environment trying to enable agents to complete?
2. **The environment:** What are \(S\), \(O\), \(A\), the transition dynamics, task distribution, and stopping condition?
3. **The evaluation:** What do you score, how do you score it, and why?
4. **The experiment:** What happened across your task suite in Langfuse?
5. **What you learned:** One interesting failure, limitation, or result that you would not have seen from a single polished demo without evaluation.
